Below are the text elements used on this site. Their texts were written on purpose to check them in the tests and to show how they are formatted.

## A heading of level two, long enough to take more than one line at every width, puts to the test the size of the section headings, the room around them and their colour

A section heading, larger than the text and well set apart from what surrounds it.

### A heading of level three, long enough to take more than one line at every width, puts to the test its colour and the room around it, below a section heading

A subheading within a section, in italics.

#### A heading of level four, long enough to take more than one line at every width, puts to the test its underline, which is all that sets it apart from the text

A minor heading, the size of the text, underlined.

## Two paragraphs, one after the other, put to the test the line height and the space between them, even when the reader asks for more spacing

Running text, with accessible line height and space between paragraphs.

This is the first of two consecutive paragraphs. It is written for the tests, and it is long enough to take more than one line at every width of the text column, so that the line height of a paragraph with several lines is measured, and not only that of a paragraph with a single line.

This is the second paragraph, right after the first. The space between the two must be at least one line and a half, and with the spacing a reader may ask for, the first must not run over the second. Like the first, it is long enough to take more than one line at every width of the text column.

## A bulleted list and a numbered list, whose items wrap, put to the test the markers, the indent and the space between the items

Lists, with amber markers and the same indent and spacing for both kinds.
